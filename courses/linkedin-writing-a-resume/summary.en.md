## Writing a Successful Resume: Getting Started

**Summary**: The course opens by addressing why some resumes get noticed while others are ignored. Stacey emphasizes that creating an effective resume requires focus, actionable steps, and customization for the target job. She encourages active participation, note-taking, and following along with exercise files rather than passive skimming.  

**Example**: Instead of a generic resume, tailor content to show why you're the right fit—highlighting relevant skills that match the job description so a recruiter moves from scanning to reading.

## The Goal of an Objective Statement

**Summary**: Objectives are debated but can be valuable for career changers or those early in their careers. They should focus the reader on why you're applying and what you offer, avoiding vague or overly long statements. Stacey reviews real examples showing good vs. poor objectives.  

**Example**: A strong objective might state you're seeking a management role leveraging advanced skills and experience, while a weak one lists responsibilities better suited for a qualifications summary.

## Your Summary of Skills / Qualifications Section

**Summary**: Also called a professional summary, this highlights key skills tailored to the job. Use bullets or columns (avoid dense paragraphs). Pull keywords from job descriptions and similar postings; include aspirational skills to show growth potential.  

**Example**: For a tech recruiting role, list "expert tech recruiter" alongside years of experience rather than generic two-word phrases.  

**[Ask AI: Summary of Skills Section](https://alisol.ir/?ai=Your%20Summary%20of%20Skills%20Section%7CStacey%20A.%20Gordon%7CWriting%20a%20Resume)**

## Showcase Professional Work Experience

**Summary**: This is the heart of the resume. Use a standard format: company name and location, job title, dates, and at least 3 bullet-point responsibilities per role. Quantify achievements with numbers and focus on impact, stability, and leadership. Start drafting this section first to make others easier.  

**Example**: Instead of "managed team," say "trained 15 team members, improving efficiency by 25% year-over-year." Place company and title on the left, dates on the right.

## How to Present Your Education

**Summary**: Place education toward the bottom if you have experience; higher if you're a recent grad or changing fields. Include anticipated graduation dates honestly, relevant coursework for incomplete degrees, and omit high school unless necessary. Be transparent to avoid issues.  

**Example**: For an unfinished degree, list attendance dates and highlight relevant courses completed.  

**[Ask AI: Presenting Education](https://alisol.ir/?ai=How%20to%20Present%20Your%20Education%7CStacey%20A.%20Gordon%7CWriting%20a%20Resume)**

## Display Memberships in Organizations and Volunteer Work

**Summary**: Memberships show initiative and industry interest; volunteer roles demonstrate leadership and skills. Use separate sections—memberships for dues-paying involvement, volunteer for active titles and responsibilities. Choose relevant ones only.  

**Example**: Founding a professional chapter and running events belongs in volunteer experience, not just memberships.  

**[Ask AI: Memberships and Volunteer Work](https://alisol.ir/?ai=Display%20Membership%20in%20an%20Organization%20and%20Volunteer%20Work%7CStacey%20A.%20Gordon%7CWriting%20a%20Resume)**

## Add a Technical or Computer Skills Section

**Summary**: Essential for admin, operations, IT, and technical roles. List specific software, systems, and tools. Spell out acronyms. This differs from the qualifications summary by being comprehensive.  

**Example**: Include QuickBooks, Mac/PC proficiency, or programming languages even if not required for the exact job, to show versatility.  

**[Ask AI: Technical Skills Section](https://alisol.ir/?ai=Add%20a%20Technical%20or%20Computer%20Skills%20Section%7CStacey%20A.%20Gordon%7CWriting%20a%20Resume)**

## Customize Identifying and Contact Information

**Summary**: Use city/state only for location. Choose professional names, emails, and voicemails. One phone and email suffice. Add LinkedIn or portfolio only if recruiter-ready.  

**Example**: Avoid unprofessional email addresses or cute voicemail greetings during job search.  

**[Ask AI: Contact and Identifying Information](https://alisol.ir/?ai=Customize%20Identifying%20Information%20and%20Optimal%20Contact%7CStacey%20A.%20Gordon%7CWriting%20a%20Resume)**

## Include Keywords and Tailor Your Resume

**Summary**: Recruiters spend seconds scanning—use keywords from job descriptions liberally, especially in experience bullets. Tailor education, achievements, and content to match requirements. Quantify everything.  

**Example**: Research similar postings on Glassdoor or company sites to find additional keywords.  

**[Ask AI: Keywords and Tailoring](https://alisol.ir/?ai=Include%20Keywords%20and%20Tailor%20Education%7CStacey%20A.%20Gordon%7CWriting%20a%20Resume)**

## Use Achievements, Awards, and Stand Out

**Summary**: Highlight measurable accomplishments early in experience sections or in a dedicated achievements area. Awards upgrade your profile if relevant.  

**Example**: "Increased department productivity by 20%" or "Number one in sales across the US for Q1-Q2."  

**[Ask AI: Achievements and Awards](https://alisol.ir/?ai=How%20to%20Use%20Achievements%20and%20Awards%7CStacey%20A.%20Gordon%7CWriting%20a%20Resume)**

## Job Search Strategies: Interviews, Networking, and Follow-Up

**Summary**: Use your resume in interviews, networking, and informational conversations. Prepare references, send thank-you notes (email + handwritten), manage online presence, and follow up professionally. Adopt a proactive approach by identifying target companies and contacts.  

**Example**: Send a handwritten thank-you note immediately after an interview; use LinkedIn thoughtfully and monitor your online reputation.
